Design a vertical test jig, built from 80/20 extrusion and 3D-printed components, for the SBIR drill/iris core cutter: it holds a rock core or simulated core upright while the spinning cutter descends over it, actuates the iris mechanism to sever the core and retain the piece, and retracts. The drill is being redesigned from 6 in tubing (6.00 in OD, 5.75 in ID, 1/8 in wall, before flights) in place of pipe; the cutting face that sets borehole and core diameter is out of scope, because the jig tests prefabricated cores (supplied by our subcontractor, the Colorado School of Mines) and does not drill them. Cores are nominally 100 mm, and a collet system accommodates 90 to 105 mm with a separate collet per diameter. The base resembles a Christmas tree stand: a threaded boss rises from it to seat the collet, and a very large wing nut tightens the holder and clamps the collet and core. Initial work will cover the base, core holder, wing nut, and a family of collets spanning the diameter range, with the CR-Agents team generating new collet models as needed; a subsequent step will address the drill-holding upper assembly, to be defined jointly once the base is reviewed. Deliverables are 3D models of every jig component and a concise technical report written as a cross between an executive summary and an engineering-school project, drawing on the SBIR proposal folder for cutter geometry and requirements.
